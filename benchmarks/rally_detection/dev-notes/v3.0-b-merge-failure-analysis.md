# v3.0-B merge failure analysis

This note records the pre-change analysis of the nine v3.0-A2 merge
collisions. It uses the committed 142-rally manifest, a fresh A2 run with the
same three ROIs, the saved coarse `splitChecks`, and 8 fps local inspection
only around the ten boundaries inside those merge candidates. It contains no
source paths or player names.

## Summary

- A2 reproduces 110 TP, 9 merges, 10 fragmented GT, 55 official unmatched,
  and 17/27 short recall.
- The nine merges contain ten GT-to-GT boundaries because benchmark 02 GT
  3/4/5 share one candidate.
- Four boundaries have a usable low-evidence valley or restart pattern. Three
  more are plausible only with local dense validation. The remaining three
  are continuously bridged or structurally ambiguous.
- None of the nine remaining merge boundaries passed A2's anti-merge
  exception. Rejections were caused by brief valleys, shallow valleys,
  low split confidence, a short child side, or the candidate being outside
  the long-candidate guard's scope.

## Practical categories

1. **Clear idle gap:** benchmark 02 GT38/39.
2. **Weak/brief but state-separable:** benchmark 01 GT28/29 and GT33/34;
   benchmark 02 GT3/4 and GT34/35.
3. **Continuous motion/background bridge:** benchmark 02 GT4/5, GT6/7, and
   GT42/43.
4. **True ambiguity / insufficient evidence:** benchmark 03 GT9/10 and
   GT29/30.

This classification deliberately leaves more than four possible boundaries:
the implementation should use the same evidence rules everywhere and may keep
an apparently plausible boundary if dense validation is incomplete.

## Fragmentation risks

A2 already has ten fragmented GT: benchmark 01 GT6, 32, 41, 45, 46, 48, and
54; benchmark 03 GT11, 28, and 29. Broadly lowering the valley duration or
split-confidence thresholds would put all ten at risk. Benchmark 03 GT29 is
the highest-risk case because its later fragment is also the candidate merged
with GT30. A safe B implementation therefore needs:

- ending-state hysteresis rather than a one-frame threshold crossing;
- bilateral evidence on both sides of a split;
- a local dense check for ambiguous coarse boundaries;
- a minimum child duration/evidence guard, with an exception only for an
  independently validated rescue-quality short rally;
- deterministic, non-overlapping output and no automatic union of a rescue
  candidate across a state-supported gap.

## Design implication

The coarse timeline is adequate for proposing boundaries but not for deciding
all of them. B should introduce an explainable `idle -> active -> ending ->
idle` state trace over the existing 2 fps evidence. Persistent low evidence
followed by a bilateral restart can split immediately when clear; ambiguous
boundaries should request one small 8 fps window and rerun the same structural
decision with dense evidence. Audio may raise or lower confidence but must not
create or veto a split on its own.
